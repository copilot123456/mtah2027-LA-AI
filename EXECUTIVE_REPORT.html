🖥️ One-screen summary

The challenge

Label every frame of a full football match with which team has the ball (or "dead ball" / "no answer"). The ranking score does not care about single frames — it counts possessions (stretches where one team keeps the ball) and matches them against the truth. Mess up the stretches, lose the score.

Our answer

A 4-stage "referee with memory": read the ball's position → weigh how close each team is with confidence → walk the whole half with a decoder that hates flickering → write the file. Every knob was tuned on 7 other matches (never on this one — the rules forbid it).

Where we are

✅ Pipeline complete and frozen · ✅ independently audited (bugs found & fixed, nothing changes the output) · ✅ 84 tests · ⏳ one "guarded look" at the official scorer and two experiments left, then freeze Nov 14.

Is it good?

Externally we score 0.2759 (95% CI 0.258–0.293) on labels that are harder than the real task, with a wide stable plateau and no over-fitting (audit-verified). The honest verdict on the official scale arrives with one guarded measurement — our band expectation is B (see Results).

⚽ The challenge, explained with zero prior knowledge

What is being asked

Imagine a football match recorded by a computer that tracks 22 players and the ball, 25 times every second. For this match that is 199,319 little snapshots (frames). For each one, our program must say:

LabelMeaningIn plain words
HHome in possessionBarcelona keeps the ball (alive)
AAway in possessionAthletic keeps the ball (alive)
DDead ballOut of play: throw-in, foul, halftime…
XNo answer"I don't know" — allowed, with a cost

Why it is genuinely hard

41% invisible ball
On the most important frames the ball is missing from the tracking (crowds, camera angles). Our program must cope with the ball disappearing for 10+ seconds at a time.
Ghost players
The tracker gives 46 slots for 22 players. Slots appear, vanish and swap people. We can only reason about teams, never individuals.
No peeking
The rules ban hand-labeling, training on this match, and checking our output against the video. Every parameter must come from other matches or from physics.

How it is scored — the part that shapes everything

The ranking metric is called spellF1. It does not count correct frames. It counts possessions — a possession is a maximal stretch where one team has the ball — and matches our stretches against the truth, stretch by stretch:

  • Stretches that overlap enough (30–70% depending on the threshold) = hit;
  • A missed possession (truth has it, we don't) or an invented one costs points;
  • Being 1 frame late is free; being 1 second late costs a third of the point;
  • Flickering is the killer: if one true possession gets chopped into pieces by our guesses, several pieces may fail to match anything;
  • X (abstain) is filled with your previous answer — so going quiet inside a possession is harmless, but going quiet across a hand-over bleeds the old team into the new one.
Visual example. Truth: ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓ home for 8 s, then ▓▓▓ away for 3 s.
A prediction that stays home 1 frame too long: still a near-perfect match. A prediction that flickers H A H A in the middle: both possessions may be lost. A constant "always home": score exactly zero.

macroF1 (per-frame correctness across the three classes) is reported as the tie-break — that is where "no answer" hurts you. Differences below ~0.04 on the full match are noise.

⚠️ The rules we operate under (organizers' clarification, Nov 3): no training/tuning on this match's ground truth or its video, no checking our output against the video, no uploading match data to AI services, labels must come from an algorithm. Allowed: any method, any external dataset we have rights to use, automatic checks. This is why everything below is tuned on IDSSE — 7 licensed Bundesliga matches (CC BY 4.0) — and never on the provided one.

🧠 State of the art — what exists, what we picked, and why

Before writing a line of code we researched the scientific literature and open-source practice for "who has the ball" problems. Here is the honest scorecard. Selected = in our solution; rejected = studied, tried, or deferred — with the reason.

✅ Methodologies we selected (and why)

MethodWhere it comes fromWhy it fits our scoring rule
Segment-level decoding
HMM / Viterbi family		 Hidden-Markov models of football match states are established research (e.g. copula-HMM for match dynamics, arXiv:2002.01193); Viterbi is the classic algorithm for "best sequence of states". The single biggest lever. spellF1 scores possessions, not frames — so we optimize the whole sequence at once with a penalty for switching. The naive baseline loses to flicker (137 fragments vs 21 real possessions); this fixes exactly that.
Soft nearest-neighbour evidence
kernel over distances		 Rule-based possession systems (open implementations like Tryolabs' soccer-video-analytics) use "who is closest to the ball". Take that idea but make it probabilistic: a decaying kernel instead of a hard winner, summed per team over the few nearest players — robust to the ghost-slot tracker (46 slots, identities swapping).
Confidence tempering
evidence fusion by trust tier		 Multi-sensor fusion practice: observations carry known error, so weight them by trust. The ball is missing 41% of the time. When we see it: full weight. When we don't: zero team weight — the frame then says nothing about sides, and the decoder carries the previous team. This implements the official rule "loose ball stays with the last team".
Learned-style dead-ball features
5 physics features + logistic		 Dead-ball ("alive/dead") classification is shown learnable in the literature — Borghesi et al., Expert Systems with Applications 2023 (TCN): 89.2% dead-alive accuracy, +33% over rule-based. We can't train a network without labels on this game, so we encode the same signals by hand from physics: ball out of bounds, ball at rest, near a line, long absence, players walking. Weights are a-priori; one naive re-fit was tried and rejected on evidence.
Switch gating + minimum dwell
temporal guards		 Smoothing/hysteresis is standard in activity recognition; we derived the exact thresholds from the metric's own math. A team change must be supported by raw tracking evidence within ±1.5 s, and no segment shorter than 2 s survives. Directly attacks the metric's "flicker kills you" rule while staying late-switch-friendly (late is cheap, early is expensive).
External-only tuning
7 matches · LOMO · plateau rule		 Standard sports-analytics methodology: cross-validation by match so a model never tests on what it trained on. Dataset: IDSSE (7 Bundesliga matches, CC BY 4.0, Scientific Data 2025). The rules demand it (no tuning on this match), and it is also just good science: pick the configuration that wins on average across matches, prefer a wide plateau over a lucky peak, verify nobody match drives the choice (over-fit check ≈ 0).
Missingness simulation
replay the ball-loss problem elsewhere		 Robustness testing = recreate your deployment conditions on test data. We measured how our ball disappears (150 gaps, median ~11 s) and replay that pattern onto the external matches — so tuning sees the same 41% blindness we'll face on match day.

❌ What we rejected (studied — and why not)

Deep temporal model (TCN / joint neural net)
Borghesi et al. 2023 is the closest published "who has the ball" system (86.2% frame accuracy) — but it needs supervised possession labels to train. With no labelled training data allowed on this game and a week of window, a net would either train on mismatched external labels or not at all. Kept as future work; our hand-built dead-ball term borrows its features.
Global min-cost-flow decoder
Sarkar et al., CVPRW 2019 — a beautiful way to assign possessions over a whole match. It solves the same job as our Viterbi with more machinery and no proven edge for a 3-state problem. Rejected: complexity without measurable gain.
Ball detector on video (YOLO)
Direct video possession estimation (Sensors 2026) is legitimate and could recover missing-ball frames — but it needs days of GPU work plus solving the broadcast camera's unsynchronized timeline, with no way to evaluate it externally before the freeze. Deferred: the one stretch item that never fit the schedule.
The broadcast feed
Our match was recorded twice; the TV feed has cuts, replays and no mapping back to the submission timeline. Using it means solving a synchronization problem first — dropped as a risk with no external way to validate.
Smart Tagging prior
The provided auto-tags (set pieces, phases) are allowed input, but weights for them can't be calibrated without external labels of the same kind. Default off, with an experiment ready if time allows.
The "fancy interpolator" (kinematic fill)
We built the state-of-the-art practice of interpolating missing ball positions (damped spline between anchors), then measured it on external data: −0.011 spellF1, worse on 7/7 matches. It is switched off. Evidence beat fashion.
Abstaining inside possessions (X)
The docs suggest going quiet when unsure. We proved — analytically and then on external data — that for a decoder already smoothed by penalties, X reproduces the same answer after scoring's forward-fill: zero gain, pure tie-break loss. Off by default.
Home/abstention asymmetries, per-frame tricks
Any prior derived from this match's public window would be tuning on the answer key (banned), and league-specific asymmetries don't transfer. Rejected on rules, not taste.

⚙️ How our solution works — the "referee with memory"

The whole system is one pipeline. Each stage takes the previous stage's output and adds one piece of judgement. Nothing ever looks at the answer key.

Stage 1 — Ball provenance. For every frame we record where the ball is (from the raw tracker, never modified), how many seconds old that observation is, and a flag for "outside the pitch" (a ball beyond the lines usually means a restart is coming). When the ball is simply absent, the frame is honestly marked none — no pretending.
Stage 2 — Evidence. Like a referee glancing at the scene: for each team, take its few closest players to the ball and add up a soft "closeness" score (exponential decay per metre — σ = 1.5 m after tuning). Separately, five physics signals vote on dead ball: out of bounds, standing still, on a line, long absence, players walking. The two outputs are a per-frame "opinion" over H / A / D, scaled by confidence.
Stage 3 — Decoder (the heart). Instead of deciding frame-by-frame (which flickers), the decoder walks each half keeping the best-scoring story. Switching home↔away costs points (penalty = 2), entering dead-ball costs 5, staying is free — so a switch must be earned by enough frames of contrary evidence. Two guards follow: a switch with no raw tracking support nearby is reverted (estimated/absent ball can't switch a possession alone), and any segment shorter than 2 s is merged into its neighbour. Result: coherent possessions, 0% sub-second fragments.
Stage 4 — Output. Frames outside the two halves (pre-match, halftime, after the whistle) get X — free, they're never scored. Inside play we always emit our decoded label (the analysis above showed abstaining can't help there). The file is written in the exact 199,319-row format and checked by the organizers' own validator.
Why this design wins (the three "whys"):
  1. It optimizes the actual score. Penalties + guards are a direct response to spellF1's math (flicker destroys possessions; late is cheap, early is expensive).
  2. It degrades gracefully. 41% missing ball doesn't produce guesses — confidence drops to zero and the story carries forward, which is literally the official rule for loose balls.
  3. Every knob is honest. Each of the ~15 parameters carries a provenance tag (a-priori / fixed / externally fitted, enforced by a test), and the externally fitted ones were picked on 7 other matches with a rule we wrote down before seeing results.

📁 The code — every file, explained

⚙️ Configuration — the single source of truth
config.pyevery knob, with a paper trail

One dictionary (CFG) holds every tunable number the pipeline uses — penalties, kernel width, dwell time, dead-ball thresholds… — and a matching PROVENANCE table saying where each value came from: prior (an educated starting guess, justified in one line), fixed_default (a physical fact, e.g. 25 fps), or external_fit (measured on the 7 IDSSE matches — the note carries the numbers).

Why it matters: a test (tests/test_config.py) fails the build if any parameter is undocumented or marked provided_* (a value from this match — always forbidden). This file is our proof to the judges that nothing was tuned on the answer key.

Current shipped values: switch_pen=2, dead_pen=5, sigma_m=1.5, min_dwell_s=2.0, switch_support_s=1.5 (externally fitted), fill tier 0 = off (externally rejected), x_use=False (proved useless).

🧭 Stage 0 — loading the match (src/)
src/__init__.pypackage marker

One docstring: which package this is. Exists so src imports cleanly.

src/data.pyloaders + the frame-alignment contract (166 lines)

The only file that touches the provided match files. It loads the tracking (via the kloppy library), the homography, the Smart Tagging, and converts everything into plain numpy arrays: ball positions, player positions & speeds, slot→team mapping, and two masks — which frames are graded (the two halves: 158,857 frames) and which half each frame belongs to.

The one rule it enforces: video_frame = frame_id − 1. The tracker counts from 1, the submission from 0 — getting this wrong shifts every label by one frame and quietly ruins the score. It's tested against the organizers' published numbers.

Performance detail: first load parses the 195 MB tracking file (~100 s) and caches it (gitignored data/.cache/); later loads take ~4 s so the pipeline reproduces fast.

① Stage 1 — where is the ball, can we trust it
src/ball_fill.pyball provenance tiers (164 lines)

Tags every frame with where the ball info comes from: atd (raw tracker observation), none (absent), or kinematic (an estimate). Each frame also gets age_s — how many seconds old the nearest real observation is — and an out_of_pitch flag (ball beyond the lines → a restart is likely).

Two promises, both tested: raw observations are copied bit-for-bit (we never "fix" the tracker by hand — that would be illegal annotation), and the flag is computed before anything is nulled so the dead-ball signal survives.

The star of this file — a damped-spline interpolator for short gaps — was built to state-of-the-art practice, then measured externally and switched off (fill_long_gap_s = 0): it hurt on 7/7 matches. The code stays (tested, re-enableable); the config says why it's off.

② Stage 2 — the frame's opinion
src/emission.pyH / A / D evidence model (171 lines)

Turns geometry into a per-frame "opinion" over the three states:

  • Team evidence: for each team, take its topk=3 visible players closest to the ball and sum exp(−(distance/σ)²) — distances in metres (normalized coordinates × pitch size), σ = 1.5 m (fitted). A soft version of "who is nearest", robust to the ghost-slot tracker.
  • Confidence: raw sighting = 1.0; absent ball = 0.0 → the H and A columns then become exactly equal (tested). Physics of the rules: without the ball, a frame must not flip anyone's possession.
  • Dead-ball term: sigmoid(bias + Σ weights × features) over five signals — out of pitch, at rest (<1 m/s), near a boundary, long absence, players walking. The bias is an a-priori logit(⅓); fitting it to external dead-share collapsed results (−0.085) and was rejected.

Output: an N×3 table of log-scores. Tests prove swap-symmetry (flip the teams → columns flip), duel-neutrality (equidistant → tie), and finiteness everywhere (no NaN/Inf, ever).

③ Stage 3 — the decoder
src/decoder.pyViterbi + gating + dwell — the heart (172 lines)

Labels each half as one coherent story instead of frame-by-frame votes:

  • Viterbi (classic best-sequence algorithm): walks all frames keeping, for each possible state, the best-scoring path so far. Staying is free; home↔away costs −2 log-units; entering/leaving dead-ball costs −5. A switch must be earned by sustained evidence.
  • Switch gating: after decoding, any team change that has no raw tracking frame supporting the new team within ±1.5 s is reverted — estimates and ball-absent stretches can never switch a possession alone.
  • Min-dwell: segments shorter than 2 s merge into the longer neighbour (shortest first, until stable) — flicker cannot survive.
  • Halves are decoded independently (state never crosses halftime), and frames outside them are left as X.

Proof of correctness (tests): zero penalties → identical to frame-by-frame argmax; huge penalties → exactly one segment; for short inputs the result is verified against brute-force enumeration of every possible path; and a flat (evidence-free) block carries the earlier team through it. 18 tests.

④ Stage 4 — policy, pipeline, output
src/x_policy.pythe abstention rule (76 lines)

Decides where X may appear. Shipped logic: outside the two halves → always X (never scored). Inside play → never X, because we proved that for a penalty-smoothed decoder, abstaining is forward-filled back to the exact same label (no spellF1 gain) while costing tie-break points. The alternative rule (X only in stable, low-confidence interiors) is implemented behind a flag and was measured externally: −0.011 → kept off.

src/pipeline.pythe glue — one function, end to end

run() wires the stages in order — load → ball provenance → evidence → decode → X-policy — and returns the labels plus a summary (segment counts, coverage). Everything downstream (submission, experiments) calls this one function, so there is exactly one definition of "our pipeline".

src/submit.pyCSV writer + official validator (87 lines)

Writes frame,label for all 199,319 rows in the exact format required (validates label codes explicitly — an impossible value raises instead of silently becoming D), then runs the organizers' own validator (evaluation/validate_submission.py) as a subprocess — path anchored to the file's location so it works from any directory. python -m src.submit is the single command that produces the shippable file.

src/diagnostics.pyhonest health report (91 lines)

Prints aggregate-only statistics of the provided data and our output: ball missingness, run-length distribution, coverage shares, confidence by tier, decoded segment counts. This is the "GREEN" output class — no ground truth, no per-frame values leaving the machine (a compliance rule we enforce on ourselves).

🧪 The external laboratory (tuning/) — where decisions get evidence
tuning/idsse.pythe 7 external matches (195 lines)

Loads IDSSE (7 full Bundesliga matches, CC BY 4.0) from disk (auto-download from HuggingFace if missing), adapts it to our pipeline's arrays, and — crucially — derives the "truth" labels algorithmically from the provider's own fields: ball_state → dead ball, ball_owning_team_id → which side. No human ever labels anything. Parses are cached like the match data.

tuning/simulate.pyreplay our missing-ball problem elsewhere

Takes the shape of our match's ball-loss (150 gaps, median ~11 s — a coarse, rule-compliant statistic) and stamps that pattern onto the external matches, seeded and reproducible. This way the tuner faces the same 41% blindness we will. (An upgrade to phase-correlated placement — losses concentrated at duels — is the E2 experiment in progress.)

tuning/sweep.pythe experiment engine (400 lines — our biggest file)

Runs grid searches of configuration cells across the 7 matches in parallel and scores each with the official metric code. Three stages were run (243 → 768 → 324 cells; 930 unique in the final union), each stage justified by the previous stage's marginal trends, with an integrity assertion: overlapping cells must reproduce the earlier matrix to the last digit (they did — max diff 0.00e+00).

Selection uses our pre-declared plateau rule: among cells within 0.01 of the best mean, pick the one whose worst grid-neighbour is strongest (prefer a wide plateau to a lucky spike), then verify with leave-one-match-out (LOMO) that no single match drove the choice — our over-fit gap: −0.0026 (essentially zero). Also here: the scoring helper that was later found to miss the official's X-forward-fill and fixed (audit finding B8).

tuning/post_checks.pyadoption checks with a written-down rule

Tests "should we change a frozen decision?" questions the same way every time: run the variant on all 7 matches against the frozen baseline, and adopt only if the mean gain exceeds +0.02 (below that = noise). It rejected the dead-bias re-fit (−0.085) and the X-policy (tie + macroF1 loss) — both now carry evidence in the dossier, not opinions.

Is it good? Three honest lenses

1 · Structural quality — strong.
Our file describes the match in football terms a coach would recognize: 337 possessions, mean 13.8 s (the published reference for this match: 17 in 5 minutes ≈ 14.2 s mean — observed, never used to tune), zero sub-second fragments (the naive baseline invents hundreds), dead-ball share 26.6% of play frames (reference 19.6%), both teams plausible. The shape of the answer is right.
2 · Scientific standing — research-grade.
The methodology is the same family as the published state of the art (temporal sequence decoding, soft sensor fusion, cross-validated external tuning) — with things most published systems don't bother with: pre-declared selection rules, bootstrap CIs, leave-one-out honesty checks, an independent audit and per-parameter provenance. No published system optimizes this metric on this data, so there is no SOTA number to quote — only a process to trust.
3 · Against the bar — on track, not proven.
Externally we sit mid-plateau with room above (best swept cell +0.0095, CI not tight). Whether we clear the 0.243 floor on the official window is exactly what the guarded run decides; the plan's expected band is B, with a defined playbook for C and A. Verdict today: defensible and reproducible; final rank pending measurement.
Room to improve — the known gaps.
• Under-segmentation: we find ~42% of the external possessions (best cell: 64%) — the decoder merges too eagerly on fine labels; E1 (labels aligned to the challenge's definition) tests whether that's the task or our dwell.
• Ball-loss realism: uniform simulated gaps vs real losses concentrated at duels — E2 may move the gating thresholds (plausible ±0.03).
• No video tier (stretch, GPU), broadcast unused, set-piece prior untried (E7) — all inside the gated backlog.
